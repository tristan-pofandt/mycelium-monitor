## 1. What RegTech is, how it differs from GRC, compliance software and FinTech, and its segments

### Takeaway
RegTech ("regulatory technology") is software that helps regulated organisations meet their legal obligations more cheaply and reliably. The term became official policy language in the UK in 2015: HM Treasury's March 2015 Budget tasked the FCA (with the PRA) to support it, and the FCA's November 2015 Call for Input followed. It started as a niche inside FinTech, set off by the heavy rules that came after the 2008 crisis. It has since grown into a cross-industry category that overlaps heavily with GRC software and cybersecurity.

### Cited Findings
**Origin and history**
- HM Treasury's March 2015 Budget announced that the FCA, working with the PRA (Prudential Regulation Authority, the Bank of England's bank-safety regulator), would identify ways to support "the adoption of new technologies to help firms better manage their regulatory requirements and reduce compliance costs – so-called 'RegTech'". — [Osborne Clarke](https://osborneclarke.com/insights/what-is-regtech-and-how-is-the-fca-encouraging-new-regulatory-technologies); [FCA RegTech page](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/regtech)
- The FCA issued its RegTech Call for Input (CFI) on 23 November 2015 and received more than 350 responses. Its Feedback Statement FS16/4 followed in July 2016. Respondents asked the FCA to play a bigger role, including an FCA certification scheme for RegTech. — [FCA FS16/4 (PDF)](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/feedback/fs-16-04.pdf); [Regulation Tomorrow, Nov 2015](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2015/11/fca-call-for-input-on-supporting-the-development-and-adoption-of-regtech/); [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/biometrics-id-verification/call-for-blockchain-and-biometrics-boost-to-uk-s-regtech)
- The FCA's 2018 Call for Input on "smarter regulatory reporting" (machine-readable, machine-executable rules, from its Digital Regulatory Reporting work) cites the 2015 responses as evidence that industry wanted regulators to support RegTech adoption. — [FCA CFI smarter regulatory reporting (PDF)](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/call-for-input/call-for-input-smarter-regulatory-reporting.pdf)
- Note on "coined": the FCA popularised the term and made it policy, and HM Treasury's Budget text is the earliest official use found. I found no source proving who first used the word. The usual line "the FCA coined RegTech" is a simplification. — same sources as above
- Post-2008 context [background, not verified this session]: the global financial crisis brought Basel III (bank capital and liquidity), Dodd-Frank (US, 2010), EMIR and MiFID II (EU trade reporting), plus far larger AML (anti-money-laundering) fines. Banks' compliance headcount and costs rose sharply. RegTech grew as the automation answer to that rising cost.

**How it differs from neighbouring terms (plain-language definitions, synthesised)**
- **FinTech** = technology that delivers financial services (payments, lending, neobanks). **RegTech** = technology that helps an organisation comply with rules. It began as a FinTech sub-sector, because banks were the first big buyers. Juniper Research notes that spending now "expands beyond just financial services". — [Juniper via ITWeb / The Stack](https://thestack.technology/regtech-spending-triples-by-2026)
- **SupTech** ("supervisory technology") is the regulator's side of the same coin: tools regulators use to supervise firms, for example ASIC's own data analytics. [background]
- **GRC (governance, risk and compliance)** is an older enterprise-software category (from about 2002 onward, after Sarbanes-Oxley; vendors such as RSA Archer, MetricStream, ServiceNow IRM). It covers policies, risk registers, controls testing and audit. Most taxonomies now treat GRC as one segment *inside* RegTech, while traditional GRC vendors call RegTech a subset of GRC. In practice the line is blurry. Funding trackers count GRC companies as RegTech: FinTech Global/RegTech Analyst named Protecht, an Australian GRC vendor, as the biggest RegTech deal of the first nine months of 2025. — [RegTech Analyst](https://regtechanalyst.com/?p=82030)
- **Compliance software** is the generic term. "RegTech" usually implies newer, cloud-delivered, data/AI-driven tools, often built for one regulation, while "compliance software" includes older on-premise tools.
- **Overlap with cybersecurity:** trackers now count cyber exposure and trust-management firms (Armis, Vanta, NinjaOne) as RegTech, because cyber rules (DORA, SEC, CPS 234) turned security evidence into a compliance product. — [RegTech Analyst 2025 top deals](https://regtechanalyst.com/?p=82030); [FinTech Global Q1 2025](https://fintech.global/?p=196500)

**Segment taxonomy: plain-language explanations (synthesised; there is no single official taxonomy)**
- FinTech Global's tracking counts more than 4,000 RegTech products from more than 1,300 vendors (2025). — [FinTech Global, Aug 2026](https://fintech.global/2026/08/05/over-5bn-raised-by-regtech-companies-in-2025/)
- Grand View, IMARC and Fortune Business Insights reports segment the market by application, typically "risk and compliance management, identity management, regulatory reporting, fraud management, regulatory intelligence", and by end user, typically "banking, insurance, FinTech, IT and telecom, public sector, energy, healthcare". — [Fortune Business Insights segmentation](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/segmentation/regtech-market-108305); [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/regtech-market) (segment share numbers not retrieved; see Gaps)

Plain explanations of each segment:
- **AML/KYC and identity verification (IDV):** "Know Your Customer" is the check a bank, exchange or (from July 2026 in Australia) a law firm must run to confirm a customer is who they claim to be, before taking them on. Software scans the ID document, matches a selfie to it (biometrics, with "liveness" checks to stop deepfakes), checks government databases, and for companies works out the real owners behind them ("beneficial ownership"). Example: FrankieOne (Australia) is an orchestration layer that connects banks and insurers to hundreds of ID and fraud data providers. — [Finextra on FrankieOne](https://finextra.com/newsarticle/39001/robinhood-and-monzo-founders-join-20-million-round-in-aus-regtech-frankieone)
- **Transaction monitoring:** after onboarding, every payment is screened by rules and machine-learning models for signs of money laundering or fraud (structuring, odd patterns, links to known bad actors). Alerts go to analysts, who decide whether to file a suspicious matter report (SMR in Australia, SAR elsewhere) with the financial-intelligence unit (AUSTRAC in Australia). The main pain is false positives, which can be well over 90% of alerts in legacy systems [background].
- **Sanctions and PEP screening:** checking customers and payments against government sanctions lists (UN, OFAC in the US, DFAT in Australia) and lists of "politically exposed persons" (officials at higher bribery risk) and adverse media. Name matching across alphabets and spellings ("fuzzy matching") is the core technology.
- **Regulatory reporting:** banks and insurers must send regulators large, structured data returns (capital, liquidity, trades, statistics). In Australia that means APRA Connect returns. Software pulls data from core systems, maps it to the regulator's template (often XBRL), validates it and files it. Regnology, which bought Moody's regulatory reporting business in 2026, and Vermeg's AGILE are leaders. — [FF News on Regnology/Vermeg](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/fintech/regnology-acquires-vermegs-regtech-division-agile-to-solidify-position-as-global-market-leader/)
- **Regulatory change management and horizon scanning:** rules change constantly across many regulators and countries. These tools ingest every new rule, consultation and enforcement action, summarise it (increasingly with AI), and map it to the firm's own policies and controls, so the firm knows what it has to change. Examples: CUBE, Corlytics, Regology (bought by Bloomberg Industry Group in June 2026). — [RegTech Analyst on CUBE/4CRisk](https://regtechanalyst.com/?p=83277); [Windsor Drake on Regology](https://windsordrake.com/market-intelligence/transactions/regology-bloomberg-industry-group-2026)
- **GRC and (operational) risk management:** a central system of record for risks, controls, incidents, policies, audits, third-party/vendor risk and board reporting. In Australia, APRA's CPS 230 (operational risk, in force 1 July 2025) has driven a lot of demand. Examples: Protecht (Sydney), ServiceNow IRM, Archer, MetricStream. — [RegTech Analyst](https://regtechanalyst.com/?p=82030)
- **Compliance monitoring and surveillance:** watching employee behaviour for misconduct. That covers trade surveillance (spotting insider trading or market manipulation) and communications surveillance (screening emails, chats and recorded calls for mis-selling or collusion), plus marketing-content review (Red Marker, Australia, a founding RegTech Association member). — [Finextra, RegTech Association launch](https://finextra.com/pressarticle/68691/regtech-association-launches-in-australia-with-spotlight-on-growing-eco-system)
- **Tax compliance:** calculating, filing and reporting indirect taxes (VAT/GST, sales tax), e-invoicing mandates, and cross-border reporting regimes such as FATCA/CRS and the OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax. Vendors include Avalara, Vertex and Sovos. [background]
- **ESG and climate reporting:** collecting emissions data (Scope 1 = own operations, Scope 2 = purchased energy, Scope 3 = supply chain and product use) and other sustainability data, then producing audit-ready disclosures under rules such as Australia's AASB S2 or the EU's CSRD. — [Anthesis guide to ASRS](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/au/regulations/asrs-aasb-s2/)
- **Privacy and data protection:** mapping where personal data lives, managing consent and cookie banners, handling data subject access requests, running privacy impact assessments and breach notification workflows (GDPR; the Australian Privacy Act and Notifiable Data Breaches scheme). Example vendor: OneTrust [background].
- **Cyber/IT compliance ("trust management"):** continuously collecting evidence that security controls work (SOC 2, ISO 27001, Essential Eight, CPS 234, DORA) and surfacing gaps. Vanta raised USD 150m at a USD 4.15bn valuation in 2025. — [RegTech Analyst](https://regtechanalyst.com/?p=82030)
- **Emerging: AI governance and "agentic" compliance:** tools that inventory and risk-assess a firm's AI models for the EU AI Act, plus AI agents that turn regulations into executable checks. Example: Norm Ai raised a USD 120m Series C on 7 July 2026 at a reported USD 1.2bn valuation, led by Khosla Ventures. — [Under30CEO](https://www.under30ceo.com/compliance-software-norm-ai-series-c/); [Corporate Compliance Insights, Mar 2025 round](https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/normai-raises-48-million-agentic-ai)

### Inferences
- The category boundary is set by funding trackers and market-research firms, not by any standard. That explains much of the spread in market-size numbers (Section 3).
- RegTech has moved from "FinTech for compliance teams in banks" to "compliance infrastructure for any regulated industry". Cyber, privacy, AI and climate rules now pull in buyers well outside finance.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Deloitte's RegTech Universe (company counts per segment) or the Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance's RegTech taxonomy (Cambridge SupTech Lab / "Global RegTech Industry Benchmark", 2019) in this session. Both are useful authoritative taxonomies to cite.

## 2. Who regulates what: key regulators and laws driving demand

### Takeaway
Demand is driven by a handful of global standard-setters (FATF for anti-money-laundering, the Basel Committee for banks), big EU laws (GDPR, DORA, NIS2, AI Act, CSRD) and US rules (SEC). In Australia, 2025-2026 brought a cluster of deadlines: APRA CPS 230 (July 2025), AML/CTF Tranche 2 (July 2026), mandatory climate reporting Group 2 (July 2026), the Children's Online Privacy Code (due December 2026) and a Privacy Act Tranche 2 exposure draft (August 2026). Together they make Australia an unusually active RegTech demand market right now.

### Cited Findings
**Global**
- **FATF (Financial Action Task Force):** the intergovernmental body (founded 1989) that sets the 40 Recommendations, the global AML/CTF standard, and runs peer "mutual evaluations" of countries. Australia's Tranche 2 reforms respond partly to FATF criticism that Australia did not regulate lawyers, accountants and real-estate agents (the "gatekeeper" professions). [background; primary: https://www.fatf-gafi.org]
- **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS):** sets bank capital and liquidity standards (Basel III/"Basel 3.1") and BCBS 239 (risk-data aggregation). These drive regulatory reporting and risk-data software. [background; primary: https://www.bis.org/bcbs]
- **GDPR (EU General Data Protection Regulation):** applied from 25 May 2018, with fines up to EUR 20m or 4% of global turnover. It created the modern privacy-tech market. [background]
- **DORA (EU Digital Operational Resilience Act):** applied from 17 January 2025 to EU financial entities and their critical ICT (IT) providers. It requires ICT risk management, incident reporting, resilience testing and a register of third-party IT contracts. [background; primary: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj]
- **NIS2 (EU Network and Information Security Directive 2):** member states had to transpose it into national law by 17 October 2024. It covers cyber risk management and incident reporting for "essential" and "important" entities across 18 sectors, and makes management personally accountable. Many states transposed late. [background]
- **EU AI Act:** in force 1 August 2024. Under the "Digital Omnibus on AI", the Parliament and Council reached a provisional agreement on 7 May 2026. It postpones stand-alone high-risk (Annex III: employment, education, credit scoring and so on) obligations to 2 December 2027, and product-embedded high-risk (Annex I) to 2 August 2028. The obligations are delayed, not removed, and Article 50 transparency duties still apply from August 2026. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Sidley, 22 Jun 2026](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [Euronews, 7 May 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/05/07/eu-reaches-tentative-deal-to-simplify-ai-rules)
- **CSRD (EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive):** the "Omnibus I" simplification reached provisional agreement in December 2025. It cuts scope to EU companies with more than 1,000 employees and net turnover above EUR 450m, with the amendments applying to financial years from 1 January 2027. CSDDD (supply-chain due diligence) scope rises to more than 5,000 employees and more than EUR 1.5bn turnover. — [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/the-omnibus-reaches-its-destination-csrd-and-cs3d-20/); [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.global/en/insights/articles/eu-council-and-eu-parliament-reach-an-agreement-to-simplify-sustainability-reporting-and-due-diligence-requirements/); [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/eu-parliament-approves-omnibus-agreement-to-cut-sustainability-reporting-and-due-diligence-requirements/)
- **SEC cybersecurity disclosure rules (US):** adopted July 2023. US-listed companies must disclose material cyber incidents on Form 8-K Item 1.05 within four business days, and describe their cyber risk management and governance annually (10-K Item 1C), effective December 2023. [background; primary: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-139]. Whether the SEC under its 2025-2026 leadership has proposed rolling these back was not checked (see Gaps).
- Deregulatory trend: in 2025-2026 the EU (Omnibus packages on CSRD and the AI Act) and the US have scaled back or delayed some obligations. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/the-omnibus-reaches-its-destination-csrd-and-cs3d-20/)

**Australia**
- **ASIC (Australian Securities and Investments Commission):** the corporate, markets, financial-services and consumer-credit regulator. It also enforces mandatory climate reporting under Regulatory Guide 280 (the AASB writes the standard but does not enforce it). ASIC has run RegTech events and liaison forums since about 2017 (Report 685 covers its 2019-20 RegTech initiatives, published January 2021). — [AASB FAQ](https://aasb.gov.au/research-resources/knowledge-hub/aasb-s2-knowledge-hub/aasb-s2-frequently-asked-questions/general-faqs/); [ASIC REP 685 (PDF)](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/5937756/rep685-published-20-january-2021.pdf); [ASIC 18th RegTech Liaison Forum slides](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/jo3b1hlw/asic-18th-regtech-liaison-forum-master-slides.pdf)
- **APRA (Australian Prudential Regulation Authority):** regulates banks, insurers and super funds.
  - **CPS 234 (Information Security):** in force since 1 July 2019. Requires information security capability, controls testing, and notifying APRA of material incidents within 72 hours. [background; primary: https://www.apra.gov.au]
  - **CPS 230 (Operational Risk Management):** in force from 1 July 2025. It covers operational risk, business continuity (critical operations and tolerance levels) and material service-provider management. Pre-existing service-provider contracts must comply by the earlier of renewal or 1 July 2026. A head of group must apply it across the group, including non-regulated entities. APRA finalised targeted amendments in 2026, including an exemption list for non-traditional service providers (Norton Rose Fulbright summary, May 2026; not verified against APRA's release). — [APRA CPS 230 (PDF)](https://www.apra.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-07/Prudential%20Standard%20CPS%20230%20Operational%20Risk%20Management%20-%20clean.pdf); [Allens guide](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2022/09/A-guide-to-APRAs-operational-risk-management-reforms/); [Pulse summary of 2026 amendments](https://www.pulse.bot/banking/news/apra-finalises-targeted-amendments-to-cps-230-operational-risk-management-d6af04c0-402a-4dc8-b374-6be0c3af9c81/)
- **AUSTRAC and AML/CTF Tranche 2:** the AML/CTF Amendment Act 2024 extends the regime to "Tranche 2" entities: lawyers, accountants, conveyancers, real-estate agents, trust and company service providers, and dealers in precious metals and stones. Obligations commenced 1 July 2026. Enrolment with AUSTRAC opened 31 March 2026 and was due by 29 July 2026. Entities need an AML/CTF program, customer due diligence and an AML/CTF compliance officer. Industry estimates put the regulated population at close to 100,000, up from about 19,000. Reformed obligations for existing reporting entities started 31 March 2026, with new TTR/SMR report forms from 1 July 2026 and a transition to 30 March 2029. — [Legl: "Tranche 2 is live"](https://legl.com/articles/tranche-2-is-live-what-the-1-july-start-date-means-for-australian-law-firms); [Legl enrolment deadline](https://legl.com/articles/the-clock-is-ticking-law-firms-have-until-29-july-to-enrol-with-austrac); [LexisNexis AU](https://www.lexisnexis.com/blogs/en-au/insights/austraia-aml-ctf-regime-whats-changed-and-whats-coming-in-2026); [FinTech Global, Dec 2025](https://fintech.global/2025/12/18/preparing-for-austrac-tranche-2-aml-reforms-in-2026/); [Small Business WA](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/blog/anti-money-laundering-and-counter-terrorism-new-reforms-explained). Caveat: these are law-firm and vendor sources. The Law Council of Australia (27 January 2026 submission) asked for lawyers' commencement to be delayed to 1 July 2027, and I did not confirm the outcome on austrac.gov.au. Legl's "is live" article implies it was not delayed.
- **OAIC and Privacy Act reform:**
  - Tranche 1 (Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024) created a statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy, automated-decision transparency duties and a mandate for a Children's Online Privacy Code. — [Wotton Kearney (PDF, 2024)](https://www.wottonkearney.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/What-You-Need-to-Know-%E2%80%93-The-Privacy-and-Other-Legislation-Amendment-Bill-2024.pdf)
  - The OAIC released the Children's Online Privacy Code exposure draft on 31 March 2026 (feedback to 5 June 2026) and must finalise it by 10 December 2026. — [G+T](https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/exposure-draft-of-childrens-online-privacy-code-released); [OAIC blog](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/blog/better-privacy-protections-for-children-are-coming)
  - **Tranche 2:** on 31 August 2026 the Government released a consultation paper and the exposure draft "Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026". Expected elements include a "fair and reasonable" test for handling personal information, plus tiered penalties and infringement notices. It had not been introduced to Parliament as of the sources found. — [Colin Biggers & Paisley, Sep 2026](https://www.cbp.com.au/insights/publications/privacy-amendment-(personal-data-protection)-bill-2026-exposure-draft-released-for-second-tranche-o); [Wotton Kearney, Sep 2026 (PDF)](https://www.wottonkearney.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Tranche-2-privacy-reforms-Wotton-Kearney.pdf); [Ashurst](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/data-bytes-au-key-australian-privacy-updates-so-far-in-2026/)
- **SOCI Act (Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018, amended 2021-22 and 2024):** covers 11 critical-infrastructure sectors, with risk-management programs, mandatory cyber incident reporting (12 hours for significant incidents, 72 hours for others) and asset registration, administered by the Cyber and Infrastructure Security Centre (Home Affairs). [background; primary: https://www.cisc.gov.au]
- **ACSC Essential Eight:** the Australian Cyber Security Centre's eight baseline mitigations (patch applications, patch operating systems, MFA, restrict admin privileges, application control, restrict Office macros, user application hardening, regular backups), assessed against Maturity Levels 0-3. It is mandatory for non-corporate Commonwealth entities under the PSPF and widely used as a benchmark by private firms and insurers. [background; primary: https://www.cyber.gov.au/resources-business-and-government/essential-cyber-security/essential-eight]. The Cyber Security Act 2024 also added mandatory ransomware-payment reporting (in force from 30 May 2025) [background].
- **ASRS / AASB S2 mandatory climate reporting:** phased in under the Corporations Act.
  - Group 1 (largest entities) from financial years starting 1 January 2025.
  - Group 2 from 1 July 2026. Thresholds are about AUD 200m revenue, 250 employees or AUD 500m assets (two of three), plus NGER reporters and asset owners at or above AUD 5bn.
  - Group 3 from 1 July 2027.
  - Scope 3 emissions are not required in the first year. Limited assurance applies from the first period.
  - ASIC enforces under RG 280.
  
  — [Anthesis](https://www.anthesisgroup.com/au/regulations/asrs-aasb-s2/); [Sustainability Tracker](https://www.sustainabilitytracker.com/article/aasb-s2-group-2-climate-reporting/); [BN Law](https://bnlaw.com.au/knowledge-hub/insights/mandatory-climate-reporting-keep-your-disclosures-crystal-clear); [AASB FAQ](https://aasb.gov.au/research-resources/knowledge-hub/aasb-s2-knowledge-hub/aasb-s2-frequently-asked-questions/general-faqs/). Sources differ on Group 2 threshold wording and on BN Law's "31 October 2026" lodgement date. For a 30 June year-end, the first Group 2 report covers FY ending 30 June 2027.

### Inferences
- In Australia the July 2026 Tranche 2 commencement is the single biggest new demand event. It brings tens of thousands of small professional firms with no compliance teams into AML/CTF, which favours cheap, self-serve KYC and AML-program SaaS.
- Globally, the 2025-2026 "simplification" wave in the EU (CSRD, AI Act) slows some ESG and AI-governance demand but does not remove it. Cyber/operational-resilience rules (DORA, NIS2, CPS 230, SOCI) were not rolled back and look like the steadier demand driver.

### Gaps
- Current status of the US SEC cyber disclosure rules in 2026 (any amendment or rescission) was not found.
- DORA/NIS2 dates, CPS 234, SOCI and Essential Eight details are from background knowledge, not fetched this session.
- AUSTRAC's own commencement notice was not fetched. Confirm the lawyer-commencement outcome on austrac.gov.au.

## 3. Market size, growth and the financial-services share

### Takeaway
Published estimates for the 2026 global RegTech market cluster at about USD 18-26 billion, growing at about 17-22% a year. Juniper Research's much larger "spend" figures (USD 83bn in 2023, and a projected USD 204bn by 2026 in its 2022 forecast) use a broader definition that counts in-house compliance spend on technology. Financial services is still the largest buyer, but I found no reliable public figure for its exact share.

## 4. Funding, M&A, consolidation 2023-2026 and where money is flowing

### Takeaway
RegTech venture funding bottomed out in 2023-2024 and recovered in 2025. Totals depend on the tracker: KPMG says about USD 4.9bn, FinTech Global about USD 5bn, RegTech Analyst about USD 8.5bn. Deal counts rose (about 520-546 deals). M&A in 2025-2026 has two strands. Large platforms are buying risk and identity data (ServiceNow-Armis, USD 7.75bn, closed April 2026). Specialists are consolidating regulatory-intelligence and reporting tools (CUBE, Corlytics, Regnology, Bloomberg-Regology). Money is flowing to AI-native or "agentic" compliance, cyber and trust management, and identity/fraud.

## 5. The Australian RegTech ecosystem

### Takeaway
Australia is an outsized RegTech hub. BCG (2020, with the RegTech Association) ranked it the world's third-largest, with about 80 of about 600 RegTechs globally and more than 65% of the Asia-Pacific RegTech market. In 2025 Australian RegTechs raised about USD 280m (4th globally), mostly the Protecht deal. The RegTech Association (founded 2017) is the industry body, and ASIC runs a long-standing RegTech liaison program. The 2026 wave of local regulation (Tranche 2, CPS 230, climate reporting, privacy) gives local vendors a home-market tailwind.

### Cited Findings
- **The RegTech Association:** a non-profit launched in Sydney in 2017 with 13 founding members, including Red Marker, GRC Solutions, Arctic Intelligence, Verifier, Dynamic GRC, Simple KYC, Cynopsis Solutions, AML Accelerate, Complii and Identitii. — [Finextra](https://finextra.com/pressarticle/68691/regtech-association-launches-in-australia-with-spotlight-on-growing-eco-system); [Arctic Intelligence blog](https://arctic-intelligence.com/insights/media/blog-coverage-start-ups-form-regtech-industry-association)

### Inferences
- Tranche 2 is a large greenfield market for Australian AML/KYC vendors, but the buyers (small law, accounting and real-estate firms) are price-sensitive and have no compliance staff. Self-serve, cheap and bundled products should win.
- Australia's funding is concentrated: one deal (Protecht) is about the whole 2025 total, so year-to-year Australian funding figures are volatile and should not be read as a trend.
